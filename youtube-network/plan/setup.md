# Setting up the channels: what YouTube's own rules require

Every rule here was read on YouTube's help pages on 16-17 Nov 2026 by the rules team and kept by its skeptic, or read
directly while writing this (marked ✓). Full findings with quotes: `results/rules-review.json`.

## Before creating any channel (the Google Account)

1. **Turn on 2-Step Verification, with a passkey.** All 13 channels hang off one Google Account, so losing it loses
   everything. Add a recovery phone and email and keep backup codes on paper. Stay the primary owner of every Brand
   Account, and never delete the Google Account: deleting it deletes every channel.
2. **One account may hold up to 100 channels**, but YouTube lists "Add additional channels to your account" as an
   **advanced feature** ✓ (support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437). Advanced features come from channel history, or
   right away from ID or video verification, which "isn't available to all creators" ✓. So creating the second and
   later channels may be blocked on a new account. If YouTube offers ID or video verification under Studio → Settings
   → Channel → Feature eligibility, use it. If not, build history on the first channel and add the others later. Never
   try another route around the block.

## Verification (needed before the first real upload, not to create a channel)

3. **A phone number can verify only 2 channels per year** ✓ (answer/171664). Verification unlocks videos over 15
   minutes and custom thumbnails ✓ (answer/9890437), so every long-form channel needs it before launch. Verify the first
   two channels to launch with the main number. Later channels need another number the owner really holds and will
   keep, or must wait a year. Never use borrowed, temporary or bought numbers: it is an anti-abuse limit. Keep a list of
   which number verified which channel and when.

## Each channel, at creation

4. **Audience.** Studio → Settings → Channel → Advanced settings. Channels 1-9 and 11-13: "No, set this channel as not
   made for kids. I almost never upload content that's made for kids." Channel 10: see the open decision below. YouTube
   holds the creator legally responsible for this setting under COPPA (answer/9528076).
5. **Comments.** Turn on Strict moderation (Basic at least), hold comments with links, and block the words scam comments
   use (WhatsApp, Telegram, "account manager", "invest with", "DM me"). The money, legal and crime channels attract
   impersonation scams.
6. **Brand look.** No logo shapes, colours or names that echo an existing company, product or channel (impersonation
   policy, answer/2801947).

## Each upload, later

7. **"AI use" in Attributes, every time.** Yes for photorealistic AI people, places or events, realistic synthetic
   voices, or AI music; No for illustrated or diagram visuals with the owner's own voice (or a clone of it) and human-made
   or licensed music (answer/14328491). Disclosure alone doesn't limit reach or earnings.
8. **No AI persona gives advice.** Channels presenting an AI as a human expert advising on health, law, money or politics
   cannot monetize at all (answer/1311392). The narrator explains; it never tells viewers what to do.
9. **Nothing templated.** Same music, same look and an AI-read AI script across many videos is YouTube's own example of
   spam that can end a channel (answer/2801973). A person researches and writes, or substantially rewrites, every script.
10. **One strike can reach the others.** Strikes "may contribute to monetization suspension on all your related
    accounts" (answer/1727191). Keep a licence record for every piece of music, footage and image. Never use another
    channel to get around a restriction.
11. **Money, later:** one AdSense for YouTube account for every channel, set up when the first channel applies.

## Open decision for the owner

**Channel 10 (wordless animated Shorts about tiny characters) is, as designed, made for kids.** The rules team found it
meets YouTube's made-for-kids factors (animated characters, simple stories, appeal to children). Set as made for kids,
YouTube turns off personalised ads, comments, the notification bell, memberships, Super Thanks and saving to playlists.
Calling it "not made for kids" while it is made for children would break COPPA, which carries legal penalties. The
choice is between keeping it as a kids' channel with those limits, or redesigning it for adults (a different look,
humour and stories) so that "not made for kids" is true.
