# Until Someone Talked: 30 candidate cases

Checked 17 Nov 2026. Every case was searched on CourtListener (search pages read with `readpage.py`; queries in the
table notes) and scouted on YouTube with `ytscout.mjs --period year` ("<case> documentary"; raw data in
`scout-cases.json`, two re-runs in `scout-extra.json`).

How to read the scores (0-10):

- **Docs**: what the CourtListener check found (published opinions and dockets naming the case), plus other primary
  material known to exist (DOJ releases on justice.gov, FBI files, trial records, books by participants). A low score
  can mean the records exist but are not on CourtListener (state trials before the 1990s, Canada, the UK); that is
  said in the notes.
- **Story**: characters, turns, a betrayal or someone who talked, an investigation, a payoff.
- **Room**: YouTube coverage this year, reversed. 10 = nothing serious exists; 0 = several strong videos in the last
  months. (The scout only sees videos uploaded in the last 12 months; big TV or streaming documentaries are noted
  separately.)
- **Risk** (living people): Low / Medium / High. High means living, private or protected people (witnesses in
  protection, guards, people never convicted), active litigation or a club or person known to sue.
- **Ads**: advertiser suitability if told without gore: Good / Fair / Poor.

"Conv." = convicted; "alleg." = allegation only. Nothing below is a finding unless a court record is named.

## Notes per case

### 1. Great Brink's Robbery, Boston, 17 Jan 1950
- **CourtListener** (`"Brink's" robbery 1950 O'Keefe`, 9 opinions): the 1953-54 First Circuit appeals of witnesses held
  in contempt for refusing to answer the federal grand jury about the robbery: *Maffie v. United States*, 209 F.2d 225;
  *O'Keefe v. United States* (three cases), 209 F.2d 223; *Daly v. United States*, 209 F.2d 232; *Hooley v. United
  States*, 209 F.2d 234; *Carlson v. United States*, 209 F.2d 209 (all 1st Cir. 1954). The state robbery
  trial and its aftermath: *Commonwealth v. Geagan*, 339 Mass. 487 (1959); *Geagan v. Gavin*, 292 F.2d 244 (1st Cir. 1961);
  *Richardson v. Commonwealth*, 355 Mass. 112 (1969). Other primary: FBI "Famous Cases" file on the robbery; O'Keefe's
  as-told-to book with Bob Considine, *The Men Who Robbed Brink's* (1961); Noel Behn, *The Big Stick-Up at Brink's!*
  (1977), from interviews with surviving robbers; contemporary Boston Globe and wire reports.
- **Story**: eleven men, months of rehearsal, a robbery with no shots fired, then six years of silence. The federal
  grand jury gets nowhere (the contempt cases are the record of men refusing to talk). One of the crew, O'Keefe, is
  jailed on an unrelated burglary, feels cheated of his share, is shot and wounded in 1954 (reported as an attempt
  arranged by others in the crew; the shooter was the hired gunman Elmer "Trigger" Burke), and in January 1956 he
  talks, days before the state's six-year deadline to charge. Eight men convicted and given life (reported; the appeal is *Geagan*: confirm the sentences there);
  little of the money was ever found. To verify before scripting: the exact limitation date and the reward figures.
- **YouTube**: Sagamo "The Biggest Heist In U.S. History" 1.68M (103K subs, 19 min, 7 months old); Folio "The Heist So
  Perfect It Fooled the FBI for 6 Years" 353K (AI art); Daring Heists 2K. All tell it as a heist. Nobody tells it as
  the story of the silence and the man who broke it, with the grand-jury records.
- **Risk**: all principals dead (last, Maffie, 1988). **Ads**: good (no on-screen violence needed).

### 2. Biloxi: the Sherry murders (1987) and the Angola "lonely hearts" ring
- **CourtListener**: *United States v. Sharpe*, 193 F.3d 852 (5th Cir. 1999) (Nix, Halat, Sharpe and others; the
  opinion describes the prison scam and the motive); *United States v. Nix* (S.D. Miss. 2000). Other primary: the 1991
  federal wire-fraud trial and the 1997 racketeering trial; Sun Herald and Clarion-Ledger reporting.
- **Story** (from the Fifth Circuit's account): Kirksey Nix, serving life at Angola, ran a mail-fraud scheme that
  conned men answering personal ads; Biloxi club owner Mike Gillich and lawyer Peter Halat handled the money; when
  money went missing from Halat's trust account he blamed his former law partner, Judge Vincent Sherry, whose wife
  Margaret was a reform-minded Biloxi politician. The Sherrys were murdered at home in September 1987. Gillich became a
  witness in 1994 (reported); the 1997 trial convicted Nix, Halat and the gunman Thomas Holcomb. A lawyer who had been
  mayor of Biloxi (reported) convicted over his partner's murder is the turn nobody expects. The scam's victims were defrauded
  gay men: tell it without mockery.
- **YouTube**: "The Deadliest Gang You've Never Heard Of | Dixie Mafia" 154K (33 min); Street Files 11K; a Spanish
  re-dub of an FBI Files episode 89K. Room for the definitive, sourced version.
- **Risk**: Nix alive (in federal prison); Halat's status to check; the Sherrys' daughter has spoken publicly. Halat
  has always denied planning the murder (his appeals say so): state his denial. **Ads**: fair.

### 3. The Westies, Hell's Kitchen (1970s-1988)
- **CourtListener**: *United States v. Coonan*, 938 F.2d 1553 (2d Cir. 1991); *United States v. William Bokun* (2d Cir.
  1995); *Coonan v. Roberts* (N.Y. App. Div. 1979); *Shannon v. Artuz* (S.D.N.Y. 1997). Other primary: the 1987-88
  federal RICO trial (S.D.N.Y.); the National Registry of Exonerations' pre-1989 entry on Featherstone's vacated
  state murder conviction (law.umich.edu); T.J. English, *The Westies* (1990).
- **Story**: Jimmy Coonan's crew takes Hell's Kitchen and allies with the Gambino family. His right-hand man Mickey
  Featherstone is convicted in 1986 of a 1985 murder he says he didn't commit; he concludes his own crew set him up,
  goes to prosecutors, his wife records conversations, the conviction is vacated and his four weeks of testimony
  (reported) help convict Coonan, who gets 75 years. A man who did kill people, freed because he was framed for the
  one he didn't: hard, true, morally complicated.
- **YouTube**: 631K (24 min, uncited), Concrete Kingdoms 189K, Mafia Talks 155K. **MGM+ series "The Westies" (2026)
  trailer: 9.0M views**: rising search interest for the real story.
- **Risk**: High. Coonan alive (BOP mandatory release date 2030; sought release in 2023), Featherstone alive (born 1948,
  witness protection reported). Use only court record and published reporting. **Ads**: fair (killings and a
  dismemberment in the trial record: describe, never show).

### 4. Operation Family Secrets, Chicago Outfit (1998-2009)
- **CourtListener**: *United States v. Ambrose*, 668 F.3d 943 (7th Cir. 2012): a deputy U.S. marshal convicted of
  leaking information about the protection of the cooperating witness Nicholas Calabrese. The main Family Secrets
  appeal (Calabrese Sr., Marcello, Lombardo, Schiro, Doyle) did not come up in my query (one retry failed on
  CourtListener's rate limit); find it before scripting. Other primary: the 2005 indictment and U.S. Attorney's
  press release (justice.gov); FBI's "Family Secrets of the Murderous Kind" (2007); Judge Zagel's sentencing record;
  Frank Calabrese Jr.'s book.
- **Story**: in 1998 Frank Calabrese Jr., in federal prison with his father, writes to the FBI offering to help keep
  him locked up, then wears a hidden recorder in the prison yard; his uncle Nick, confronted (as reported) with DNA
  evidence in a 1986 murder, becomes the first made member of the Outfit to testify; 2007 trial; five convicted,
  Frank Sr. given life for racketeering (the judge found 13 murders proven). Then the man protecting Nick leaks.
- **YouTube**: National Geographic compilation "Mob Betrayals from New York to Chicago" 1.53M (TV, covers several
  cases); Street Files 24K. No dedicated, sourced long-form version.
- **Risk**: Medium. Frank Jr. is a public author; Nick Calabrese's whereabouts are protected; Frank Sr. died 2012;
  Lombardo died in prison. **Ads**: good.

### 5. El Rukn / Jeff Fort (1960s-1990s)
- **CourtListener** (33 opinions for "El Rukn"): *United States v. McAnderson, Hawkins, Fort, Knox and Cranshaw*
  (7th Cir. 1990), the appeal from the 1987 conviction for conspiring to commit terrorism for Libya; *United States v.
  Boyd* (N.D. Ill. 1993 and 1994) and *United States v. Jeff Boyd*, 55 F.3d 239 (7th Cir. 1995), new trials ordered
  because prosecutors hid that cooperating El Rukn witnesses used drugs and had other favours in custody; *United
  States v. Burnside* (N.D. Ill. June 1993); *United States v. Fort* (N.D. Ill. 1996); *People v. Fort* (Ill. App.
  1993); *Nathson Fields v. City of Chicago* (7th Cir. 2020). Other primary: the 1968 U.S. Senate hearings on the
  Blackstone Rangers' antipoverty grant (to verify); Chicago Tribune and Chicago Reader reporting.
- **Story**: a South Side youth group becomes the Blackstone Rangers, then El Rukn; federal money, a Senate hearing,
  a Libya plot caught on wiretaps, life sentences built on insiders who talked, and then the insiders' deals unravel
  the cases in court. "Someone talked" twice, in opposite directions.
- **YouTube**: Hood Archives "Larry Hoover vs Jeff Fort" 371K; FBI Investigates 56K; a Spanish FBI Files 61K. The
  informant scandal is barely told.
- **Risk**: High. Fort alive (federal prison); many former members alive; Nathson Fields was exonerated: never imply
  guilt where courts reversed. **Ads**: fair (terrorism plot, murders).

## Why these top picks (not fame)

The top of the list is where three things meet: court records that let every claim be shown on screen, a real
person who talked (or refused to, then did) as the turn of the story, and no strong video this year. Famous cases
(Lufthansa, Hudson, Dunbar, Supreme Team, Flores twins) score high on story but have no room; dark, ad-unfriendly or
high-risk cases (Aryan Brotherhood, BGF jail, Latin Kings) are passed even with good records.
