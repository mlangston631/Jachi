# Until Someone Talked: stories, first uploads, series and beat sheet

Built on cases.md (scores, court records) and market.md (what wins). Conventions used throughout:

- **[court]** = stated in a court opinion found on CourtListener (named). **[reported]** = contemporary or later
  journalism, or a participant's own book, to be checked against a second source. **[lead]** = only found on Wikipedia
  or in a web-search summary so far: must be confirmed in a primary source before it is scripted. **[alleg.]** =
  allegation, never proven in court. **[claim]** = a participant's own account, which the narration must attribute.
- No invented dialogue. Quotes may only be read from a record or a published book, with the source on screen.
- Every recreation is labelled on screen ("Recreation") and shows no identifiable face.
- Titles follow market.md: one concrete promise, the person who talked or the turn in the title, no "Part 2" in titles.

## Top 10, ranked by storytelling potential

### 1. Brink's, Boston, 1950: the six-year silence
- **Title**: The Brink's Gang Kept Quiet for Six Years. Then One of Them Was Shot.
  - Alt: Six Years of Silence: The Brink's Robbery and the Man Who Talked
  - Alt: America's Biggest Cash Robbery Was Solved by a Grudge
- **Thumbnail**: archival 1950s mugshot of Joseph "Specs" O'Keefe on the right, half in shadow; left, the Brink's
  building at night (archival photo), a hand-drawn line connecting them; text "6 YEARS". Alt: a 1956 calendar page
  with January 17 circled in red, over a faded newspaper front page; text "5 DAYS LEFT" (only if the deadline
  arithmetic is confirmed).
- **Summary**: on 17 January 1950 masked men robbed Brink's at 165 Prince Street, Boston, of about $1.2 million in
  cash and more in cheques and securities [court: *Richardson v. Commonwealth*, 1969; *Commonwealth v. Geagan*, 1959].
  For years nobody could be charged. A federal grand jury called witnesses who refused to answer, and their contempt
  cases went to the First Circuit [court: *Maffie*, *Daly*, *Hooley*, *Carlson* v. United States, 1954]. One member
  of the crew, O'Keefe, in prison on another burglary, came to believe he had been cheated of his share; he was shot
  and wounded, by a hired gunman, in an attempt reported as arranged by others in the crew [reported; FBI file]. In
  January 1956 he talked, days before the deadline to charge them [lead; confirm in FBI file and 1956 press]. Eight men
  received life sentences, upheld on appeal [court: *Geagan*, 1959]; most of the money was never found [reported].
- **Central mystery**: how did a crew of small-time Boston thieves keep the biggest cash robbery in the country secret
  for six years, and what finally broke it?
- **Cold-open hook**: 1954 [reported]: one of the robbers survives being shot by a hired gunman and still keeps the
  code. In January 1956, with the clock on the case almost out, he decides to talk [lead: date per FBI file].
- **Turning points**: (1) the robbery, no shot fired; (2) the getaway truck found cut up [reported]; (3) the grand
  jury that got nothing (court records of men refusing to answer); (4) O'Keefe and a crew member jailed in
  Pennsylvania on another burglary [lead]; (5) O'Keefe's demands for his share and the attempts on his life [reported;
  O'Keefe's book]; (6) the decision to talk, January 1956; (7) arrests days before the deadline; (8) the 1956 trial,
  life sentences, appeals.
- **Why watch**: a heist everyone half-knows, told from the inside of the silence, with the real records on screen;
  the payoff is human (a grudge, not police genius) and it is the channel's thesis.
- **Shape**: standalone.
- **Key sources**: *Commonwealth v. Geagan*, 339 Mass. 487 (1959); *Geagan v. Gavin*, 292 F.2d 244 (1st Cir. 1961);
  *Richardson v. Commonwealth*, 355 Mass. 112 (1969); *Maffie v. United States*, 209 F.2d 225, and companion cases
  209 F.2d 209-234 (1st Cir. 1954); FBI "Famous Cases: Brink's Robbery" and the FBI Vault file; O'Keefe with Bob
  Considine, *The Men Who Robbed Brink's* (1961); Noel Behn, *The Big Stick-Up at Brink's!* (1977); Boston Globe,
  18 Jan 1950 and Jan 1956; NYT obituary of O'Keefe, 28 Mar 1976.

### 2. The Westies: framed by his own crew
- **Title**: His Own Crew Framed Him for Murder. So He Took Them All Down.
  - Alt: The Westies: The Hell's Kitchen Gang Undone by Its Second in Command
  - Alt: The Real Westies: What the Trial Revealed
- **Thumbnail**: archival photo of Mickey Featherstone (press photo, 1980s) large, a torn strip showing Jimmy Coonan
  behind him; text "FRAMED". Alt: Hell's Kitchen tenement street at night (licensed archival), a courtroom sketch inset,
  text "HE TALKED".
- **Summary**: Jimmy Coonan's crew ran rackets in Hell's Kitchen and worked with the Gambino family [court: *United
  States v. Coonan*, 2d Cir. 1991]. His right-hand man Featherstone was convicted in 1986 of a 1985 murder he said he
  did not commit; he concluded he had been set up by his own side [reported; Exoneration Registry]. He went to
  prosecutors, his wife recorded crew members [reported], the conviction was set aside and his testimony at the
  1987-88 federal racketeering trial helped convict Coonan, sentenced to 75 years [reported; *Coonan*].
- **Central mystery**: who framed Mickey Featherstone, and why would a man who admits to killings be believed?
- **Cold-open hook**: 1986, a jury convicts a man of murder; this time, he says, he didn't do it. He knows who did.
- **Turning points**: Coonan's rise; the 1977 killing of the old Hell's Kitchen boss (Featherstone tried and acquitted
  [lead]); the Gambino alliance; the falling-out; the 1986 conviction; the tapes; the vacated conviction; the trial;
  Coonan's 75 years; 2023 release bid.
- **Why watch**: the MGM+ series has put the name in front of millions (trailer 9.0M views); this is the record behind it.
- **Shape**: two-part series (see below).
- **Key sources**: *United States v. Coonan*, 938 F.2d 1553 (2d Cir. 1991); *United States v. Bokun* (2d Cir. 1995);
  *Coonan v. Roberts* (N.Y. App. Div. 1979); National Registry of Exonerations, pre-1989 case entry; T.J. English,
  *The Westies* (1990); AP trial coverage 1987-88; S.D.N.Y. First Step Act motion, Aug 2023.

### 3. Operation Family Secrets: the son, the brother and the marshal
- **Title**: He Wrote to the FBI to Put His Father Away. Then His Uncle Talked.
  - Alt: Family Secrets: The Letter That Broke the Chicago Outfit

  as hidden in headphones), text "WIRED".
- **Summary**: in 1998 Frank Calabrese Jr., serving time with his father, wrote to the FBI offering to help keep him in
  prison and then recorded him [reported; FBI 2007]. His uncle Nick Calabrese became a cooperating witness [court:
  *United States v. Ambrose*, 7th Cir. 2012]. The 2005 indictment charged the Outfit as an enterprise; the 2007 trial
  convicted five; Frank Sr. got life [reported; DOJ]. A deputy U.S. marshal was later convicted of leaking information

  keep them alive?
- **Cold-open hook**: July 1998 [lead]: a letter leaves a federal prison in Michigan addressed to the FBI. The writer's
  father is in the same prison.
- **Turning points**: the letter; the recordings; Nick's cooperation; the indictment; the trial; the sentences; the leak.
- **Why watch**: betrayal inside a literal family, with the government's own failure as the final twist.
- **Shape**: two-part series.
- **Key sources**: *United States v. Ambrose*, 668 F.3d 943 (7th Cir. 2012); the Family Secrets appeal (7th Cir., to

### 4. Biloxi, 1987: the judge, the lawyer and the prison scam
- **Title**: A Prison Con Paid for a Judge's Murder. His Law Partner Was Convicted.
  - Alt: The Biloxi Murders: How a Scam Run from Angola Ended in a Double Killing
  - Alt: The Dixie Mafia's Last Job
- **Thumbnail**: archival portrait of Vincent and Margaret Sherry (family-released press photo only) beside a prison
  fence at Angola; text "WHO PAID?". Alt: a 1980s personal-ads page (blurred, labelled), an envelope of cash, text
  "THE SCAM".
- **Summary**: from Angola, Kirksey Nix ran a fraud on men answering personal ads; the money passed through Biloxi
  [court: *United States v. Sharpe*, 5th Cir. 1999]. When money went missing from Peter Halat's trust account, Halat
  pointed at his former partner, Judge Vincent Sherry, whose wife Margaret was a reform candidate [court: *Sharpe*].
  The Sherrys were murdered in their home in September 1987. A 1991 federal trial convicted Nix and others of fraud;
  Gillich became a witness in 1994 [lead]; in 1997 Nix, Halat and the gunman Holcomb were convicted [lead; confirm in
  *Sharpe*]. Halat denied planning the murders [court record of his appeal].
- **Central mystery**: who wanted the Sherrys dead, and why did it take ten years to prove it?
- **Cold-open hook**: September 1987: a judge and his wife are found shot dead in their Biloxi home. Ten years later
  a jury convicts the judge's former law partner, by then a former mayor of Biloxi [lead], over the killings.
- **Turning points**: the scam; the missing money; the killings; the 1991 fraud trial; Gillich turns; the 1997 trial;
  appeals.
- **Why watch**: a Southern crew few people know, an unthinkable betrayal between partners, a daughter who pushed for
  justice; room on YouTube (best English video 154K).
- **Shape**: standalone.
- **Key sources**: *United States v. Sharpe*, 193 F.3d 852 (5th Cir. 1999); *United States v. Nix* (S.D. Miss. 2000);
  Sun Herald (Biloxi) series, incl. 14 Sep 2017; Clarion-Ledger, 19 Jun 1997; WLOX, 14 Feb 2001.

### 5. El Rukn: the gang, the Libya plot and the witnesses who were paid in favours
- **Title**: The Chicago Gang That Offered to Work for Libya
  - Alt: El Rukn: How a Street Gang's Informants Brought Down Its Leaders, and Then Their Own Case
  - Alt: The Prosecution That Fell Apart: El Rukn
- **Thumbnail**: archival 1980s photo of the El Rukn building on the South Side (press), a 1986 wiretap reel
  (illustration, labelled); text "LIBYA?". Alt: courthouse exterior, red stamp "NEW TRIAL".
- **Summary**: Jeff Fort's organisation was convicted in 1987 of conspiring to commit terrorism for Libya in exchange
  for money [court: *United States v. McAnderson*, 7th Cir. 1990]. In 1991-92, federal trials convicted dozens of
  El Rukn members, many on the word of former leaders who cooperated. In 1993 judges ordered new trials after finding
  the government had failed to disclose that cooperating witnesses used drugs and had other favours in custody [court:
  *United States v. Boyd*, N.D. Ill. 1993; 7th Cir. 1995].
- **Central mystery**: how could the government win, then lose, the case against Chicago's most notorious gang because
  of the very men who talked?
- **Hook**: 1986: the FBI hears a gang leader in prison, on a phone line, talk about Libya [court: *McAnderson*].
- **Turning points**: see the three-part series below.
- **Why watch**: two betrayals that cancel each other out; a story of how informant deals can corrupt a case.
- **Shape**: three-part series.
- **Key sources**: *United States v. McAnderson* (7th Cir. 1990); *United States v. Boyd*, 833 F. Supp. 1277 (N.D. Ill.
  1993) and 55 F.3d 239 (7th Cir. 1995); *United States v. Burnside* (N.D. Ill. 1993); *United States v. Andrews*,
  824 F. Supp. 1273 (N.D. Ill. 1993); *United States v. Fort* (N.D. Ill. 1996); *Fields v. City of Chicago* (7th Cir.
  2020); 1968 Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations hearings [lead].

## First five uploads, in order

1. **Brink's** (standalone): the channel's thesis in its purest form, no living-person risk, the most ad-safe story,
   a famous name with a fresh angle and room on YouTube. It sets the promise: real records on screen, silence, then
   someone talks.
2. **Biloxi: the Sherry murders** (standalone): a different region and decade, a betrayal between partners, low
   competition. It shows the channel isn't only Boston heists, and its ending names the next upload's theme (a man
   convicted by his own side).
3. **The Westies, part 1** and 4. **The Westies, part 2**: rides the MGM+ series' demand; the first two-parter, a week
   apart, each titled as its own film. Part 1 ends on the 1986 conviction (a real cliffhanger), part 2 pays off.
   Viewers who finish part 1 have a reason to subscribe for part 2.
5. **Family Secrets, part 1**: the second series starts while the first is still pulling; the Westies' story ends
   with a mob family allied to the Gambinos, Family Secrets opens on a family that testified against itself. By upload 5
   the channel has two finished payoffs and one open loop, the shape a returning audience needs.

El Rukn (three parts) follows once the channel has a base; it is the most demanding to source and to handle.

## Three multipart series

Each part is a complete film with its own title and payoff; each ends on a real, documented unresolved development.

### Series A: The Westies (2 parts)
- **Part 1: "The Hell's Kitchen Crew That Worked for the Gambinos"** (~25 min). Origins of Coonan's crew, the rackets,
  the 1977 killing of the neighbourhood's old boss (Featherstone tried and acquitted [lead]), the Gambino alliance,
  the 1979 murder trial that collapsed after one witness died and another refused to testify [lead]. Payoff: how the
  crew became untouchable. Ends: March 1986, Featherstone convicted of the 1985 Michael Holly murder and given a long
  sentence; he says he didn't do it [reported]. Unresolved: who did.
- **Part 2: "Framed by His Own Crew: Mickey Featherstone Talks"** (~27 min). The recordings by his wife [reported],
  the vacated conviction, Bokun's case [court: *Bokun*], the 1987-88 RICO trial and Coonan's 75 years [court:
  *Coonan*]. Payoff: the crew undone by its own frame. Ends: 2023, Coonan, by then in his late seventies [lead], asks to be released; his
  mandatory release date is 2030 [lead: BOP record]. Unresolved: whether he will die in prison.

### Series B: Operation Family Secrets (2 parts)
- **Part 1: "Dear FBI: The Son Who Wired His Father"** (~26 min). The Outfit's street crews and juice loans; Frank
  Sr.'s 1995 conviction with family members [reported]; the 1998 letter; the recordings in the prison yard. Payoff:
  the son's evidence. Ends: the FBI needs a made man to make murders stick; Nick Calabrese, linked by evidence
  (reported as DNA) to the 1986 murder of John Fecarotta, decides to cooperate [reported]. Unresolved: whether a
  brother's word will hold in court.
- **Part 2: "The Brother Who Testified, and the Marshal Who Leaked"** (~28 min). The 2005 indictment naming the
  Outfit as an enterprise, the 2007 trial, the verdicts, Judge Zagel's sentences [reported; DOJ], and the deputy
  marshal's conviction for leaking [court: *Ambrose*]. Ends: the judge's warning to Nick at sentencing that the
  organisation would not forgive him [lead: quote must be read from the transcript or a contemporary report], and the
  murders in the indictment the jury could not resolve [lead]. Unresolved: the Outfit was weakened, not ended.

### Series C: El Rukn (3 parts)
- **Part 1: "From Blackstone Rangers to El Rukn"** (~25 min). The 1960s South Side, the federal antipoverty grant and
  the 1968 Senate hearings [lead], Fort's first federal conviction [lead], the name change and the fortress building.
  Payoff: how a youth gang became an organisation with a leader in prison. Ends: 1986, FBI agents listening to calls
  from a federal prison about Libya [court: *McAnderson*]. Unresolved: what the gang offered.
- **Part 2: "The Libya Plot"** (~26 min). The calls, the coded language, the weapons purchase that the FBI arranged [lead;
  read the facts from *McAnderson* before scripting], the 1987 terrorism conviction and appeal. Payoff: the first
  American street gang convicted of a terrorism conspiracy for a foreign government [reported; verify "first"]. Ends:
  insiders begin cooperating against the leaders for the murder and drug trials of 1991 [court: *Boyd*]. Unresolved:
  what they were given in return.
- **Part 3: "The Witnesses Who Wrecked Their Own Case"** (~28 min). The 1991-92 trials, the evidence that cooperating
  witnesses used drugs and had favours in custody, the 1993 rulings ordering new trials, the 1995 appeal [court:
  *Boyd*; *Burnside*; *Andrews*], and the later wrongful-conviction case of Nathson Fields [court: *Fields*]. Ends: the
  retrials and the open debate over informant deals. Unresolved: how many other convictions rested on the same men.
