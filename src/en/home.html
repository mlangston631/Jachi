Who provides your care.

Your care comes from licensed clinicians who practice through independent medical groups. Jachi Health provides the technology and administrative services. Before we open, we'll name the medical group and its clinicians here.

  • {{I:steth}}

    Licensed in your state

    Physicians, nurse practitioners and physician assistants provide medical care, and licensed therapists provide therapy. Each one is licensed in the state where you live.

  • {{I:shield}}

    Physician oversight

    Nurse practitioners and physician assistants work with a supervising physician, as Texas law requires.

  • {{I:user}}

    A clinician makes every decision

    Only a licensed clinician diagnoses, prescribes or changes your treatment. Our AI assistant never does.

  • {{I:pill}}

    Careful prescribing

    FDA-approved medications, only when they're right for you. We don't prescribe controlled substances.

  • {{I:alert}}

    Safety comes first

    If online care isn't right for you, your clinician will tell you where to go in person. For an emergency, call 911. In a crisis, call or text 988.

  • {{I:lock}}

    Your privacy

    When you become a patient, your health information is protected under HIPAA. This site doesn't use ads or tracking.

Check a license yourself

Licenses are public in Texas. You can look up any physician, physician assistant, nurse practitioner or therapist with the state board that licenses them.

How eligibility works

FDA-approved weight-loss medications are generally for adults who meet one of these BMI guidelines:

30+BMI on its own
27+BMI with a weight-related condition

BMI is a screening number, not a measure of your health. A licensed clinician looks at your full health picture before recommending any treatment.

What a clinical evaluation involves

  • Your health history. A licensed clinician reviews your health conditions, medications, allergies and any past weight-loss treatment.
  • Details they may ask for. Your current weight, your blood pressure and recent lab results, if you have them.
  • Lab tests. Some people need blood work before starting or during treatment. Your clinician will tell you if you do.
  • The medication decision. Only your clinician decides whether a medication is right for you, and which one. Not everyone is prescribed medication.
  • Ongoing care. Regular check-ins to track your progress and any side effects, and to adjust your dose.
